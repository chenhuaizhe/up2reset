System sit reminders are easy to ignore. A covered screen is not.

macOS can ping you. You swipe it away and keep typing. Up2Reset is for the hour you forget you have a body: it dims every monitor, counts down a short break, then gets out of the way.

  1. 00:00
    You start a work block. The timer stays in the menu bar, not the Dock.
  2. 58:00
    A system notification can warn you a couple of minutes early, if you want it.
  3. 60:00
    Every screen is covered. Stand up. Stretch. Write one line about what you were doing, if you use work notes.
  4. 05:00
    Confirm you moved. The overlay closes. The next block starts.

What it actually does

  • Menu bar timer Pause, continue, restart, and see today’s work time and break count without leaving the app you are in.
  • Full-screen overlay on every display Including extra monitors. Hot-plug a screen mid-break and the overlay rebuilds instead of vanishing.
  • Skip, then get called again In a meeting? Skip. It comes back after a short delay instead of disappearing for the afternoon.
  • Sleep and lock pause the clock Close the lid or lock the Mac, and the work timer waits. You should not be punished for leaving the desk.
  • Work notes on the overlay One line: what you were just doing. Useful when the next sitting block starts and your brain is empty.
  • Launch at login A sit reminder that you have to remember to open is not a sit reminder.

Free vs Pro

The free app is enough to break a sitting streak. Pro is for people who want a custom rhythm and a local record.

Free

$0

  • Full-screen overlay and countdown
  • Pre-break notification
  • Skip and remind later
  • Sleep / lock detection
  • Today’s summary

Pro

Annual or lifetime, in-app

  • Any work interval in minutes
  • Keyboard and mouse idle detection
  • More skip and warning options
  • 7 / 30 / 365 day local reports
  • Purchases go through Apple. Up2Reset never sees card data.

Nothing leaves the Mac.

No account. No analytics SDK. No third-party libraries. Idle detection uses a system idle clock, not Accessibility permission, and not a global keylogger. Privacy policy.
